**Getting Started**

**What is meal prepping?**

Meal prep is simply planning ahead and doing a little prep in advance so weekday meals are faster, easier, and less stressful. You can prep full meals, but most families do best with prepping ingredients (the “building blocks”) so you have options all week.

**Why meal prep works (especially for families)**

- Saves time on busy weeknights
- Reduces stress and “what’s for dinner?” decision fatigue
- Helps you eat more balanced meals without overthinking
- Makes it easier to stay on budget and waste less food
- Gives you flexible options when plans change

**The Simple 3-Part Meal Prep System (Made for Real Life)**

**1) Prep ingredients, not recipes**\
Instead of cooking five full meals, you prep a few mix-and-match building blocks.\
Think: one or two proteins + ready-to-use veggies + a grain or starch + a sauce or seasoning.\
When the “parts” are ready, you’re not starting from scratch every night—you’re just putting dinner together.

**2) Cook once, mix and match all week**\
One prep session turns into multiple meals. You use the same ingredients in different ways—so it feels fresh, not repetitive.\
Example: cooked chicken can become tacos one night, a salad the next, and a rice bowl later in the week. This saves time, money, and a lot of mental energy.

**3) Same meals, new flavors**\
To keep it from getting boring, you repeat easy meal “formats” (like bowls, tacos, salads, wraps), but you change the flavor with different sauces, spices, and toppings.\
Same base ingredients… new vibe. That’s how meal prep stays delicious and sustainable.

**In short:** prep a few basics, remix them through the week, and switch up flavors so your family stays interested.

**Step 3: Prep Ingredients (The Foundation Strategy)**

Instead of prepping five complete recipes, you’ll prep a few flexible staples you can mix and match.

**Your basic building blocks:**

- 3 proteins
- 3 veggies (fresh prepped + frozen counts)
- 3 fruits
- 3 carbs: whole grain /starch
- 3 sauces or flavor boosters

Balanced plate reminder (simple): protein + fiber-rich carbs + healthy fats.

**1) Protein (the “bottleneck”)**

For most families, protein is the hardest part. When it’s cooked, dinner becomes **assembly**, not cooking.

**Easy protein ideas:**

- Rotisserie chicken (shredded or sliced)
- Baked/grilled chicken breasts
- Ground turkey or ground beef (simply seasoned)
- Slow-cooker shredded chicken/beef/pork
- Meatballs (baked)
- Hard-boiled eggs
- Canned tuna or salmon
- Beans or lentils (canned or batch-cooked)
- Tofu or tempeh (baked or sautéed)
- Veggie burgers

**2) Vegetables (make them grab-and-go)**

Veggies don’t need to be fancy—just **washed, chopped, and easy to use**. When they’re ready, you’re halfway to a meal.

**Fresh veggies that prep well:**

- Bell peppers, cucumbers, carrots
- Cherry tomatoes
- Broccoli/cauliflower florets
- Zucchini/summer squash, green beans
- Onions (pre-sliced)
- Leafy greens (washed and spun)

**Frozen veggies (your weeknight best friend):**

- Broccoli, cauliflower, mixed veg
- Spinach or kale
- Peas
- Stir-fry blends

**Family tip:** Offer veggies both **raw and cooked**—different textures feel like different foods, which helps picky eaters without pressure.
